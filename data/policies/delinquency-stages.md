# Missed Payments — What Happens and When

## Overview
If an instalment is not paid by its due date, your loan becomes past due. The longer it stays unpaid,
the more serious the consequences. Paying, or agreeing a promise to pay, at any stage stops the process
from moving forward.

## Stage by stage
| Days past due | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 to 29 | Reminder calls and messages. A late fee is added if the instalment is still unpaid when your product's grace period ends. |
| 30 | The missed payment is reported to the credit bureau as late. |
| 60 | The account is passed to our collections team, who will contact you to agree a plan. |
| 90 | The loan may be declared in default. The bank may then require the full balance and consider further action. |

## Credit bureau reporting
When a loan reaches 30 days past due, we report the late payment to the credit bureau. This can affect
your credit score. Paying before you reach 30 days past due avoids this report. See the credit bureau
reporting policy.

## Collections at 60 days past due
At 60 days past due, a specialist collections officer takes over your account. They can discuss a
repayment plan, and hardship options if your circumstances have changed.

## Default at 90 days past due
At 90 days past due the loan may be declared in default. Decisions at this stage, including any legal
action or action on security such as a vehicle or a home, are made only by bank staff and never on an
automated call.

## How to stop it
Pay the overdue amount using any payment channel, or make a promise to pay within the policy window.
If you cannot pay because of hardship or you dispute the amount, tell us and we will pass you to a
member of staff.
