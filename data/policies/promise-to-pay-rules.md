# Promise to Pay

## What a promise to pay is
If you cannot pay an overdue amount today, you can tell us the date you will pay and how much. We record
this as a promise to pay and pause reminder calls about that amount until the promised date.

## The rules
- The promised date must be no more than 14 days after the day you make the promise.
- The amount must be more than zero and no more than the amount currently due.
- A partial payment can be promised. The rest of the overdue amount remains past due, and late fees and
  reporting still follow the normal timeline for it.
- One open promise is allowed per loan at a time.

## What a promise does not change
A promise to pay does not change your due dates, waive fees or stop interest. It does not stop credit
bureau reporting if the loan reaches 30 days past due before you pay.

## If a promise is broken
If the promised payment has not been received by the end of the promised date, the promise is marked as
broken. We will contact you again, within our calling rules, to follow up. Repeated broken promises may
mean the account is passed to a member of staff.

## Making a promise
You can make a promise to pay with the virtual assistant during a call, which will read the date and
amount back to you to confirm. You can also make one with any member of staff.
